# Publish → Discover → Outreach → Feedback

## Purpose

This is the default portfolio-wide cadence for moving existing research and project work into public circulation, targeted conversation, and useful feedback.

The goal is **not** to manufacture content or create new projects for the sake of posting. The goal is to periodically surface work that already exists, make it legible to people outside the repository where it originated, and route resulting criticism or insight back into the underlying work.

```text
RESEARCH / WORK
      ↓
PUBLIC SYNTHESIS
      ↓
DISCOVERY
      ↓
TARGETED OUTREACH
      ↓
FEEDBACK
      ↓
BACK INTO THE WORK
```

## Default cadence

Target: **one cycle every other day**, subject to actual work priorities.

A missed cycle does not create backlog debt. Resume with the next useful piece of existing work.

### 1. Select existing work

Choose one substantive idea, finding, framework, design decision, negative result, or body of research that has already earned enough substance to explain publicly.

Do not start a new project merely to satisfy the cadence.

### 2. Publish a readable synthesis

Write a public-facing Substack article that preserves the substance of the underlying work while making it understandable without requiring the reader to first inspect a repository, book, technical disclosure, or long research history.

Distinguish demonstrated results from proposals, hypotheses, analogies, and research agendas. Do not inflate evidence for reach.

### 3. Create a discovery surface

Publish a shorter LinkedIn version designed to create an intellectual entry point rather than reproduce the full article.

The LinkedIn post should usually communicate **one question, distinction, or implication** and point interested readers toward the deeper piece. Avoid generic promotional language.

### 4. Conduct targeted private outreach

Select a small set of people whose actual work intersects the article. Do not mass-blast and do not send every article to the same recipients.

Work approximately from broad reach toward proximity to practice:

1. major thinker, researcher, or institution;
2. national policymaker or relevant federal institution;
3. relevant congressional representative or other federal official;
4. Ohio policymaker when the issue has state relevance;
5. local government, economic-development, workforce, education, or civic leadership;
6. practitioner closest to the problem — employer, researcher, professor, tradesperson, nonprofit, community participant, or domain specialist.

The ladder is a heuristic, not a quota. Skip levels that do not fit the subject.

### 5. Tailor the reason for contact

Every private note should answer: **Why does this specific person have a reason to care about this specific piece?**

Prefer asking for criticism, feedback, or perspective when appropriate. Do not default to asking for funding, endorsement, promotion, or a meeting.

If a person, podcast, paper, conversation, or other source directly inspired the article, close the loop by sending the work back to that source and explaining the connection.

### 6. Build relationships without promotion

For difficult-to-reach people, following their work and contributing thoughtful, non-promotional comments is preferable to repeated connection requests or self-promotion.

A useful comment should add something: a distinction, implication, counterexample, question, evidence, or connection. Do not repeatedly redirect another person's audience to this portfolio.

### 7. Record and learn

For each cycle, preserve enough information to reconstruct what happened:

```text
article / artifact
tesis or core question
publication date
recipient
why this recipient
channel
date sent
response
useful criticism or insight
follow-up, if any
change made to underlying work, if any
```

Private correspondence details should remain in an appropriate private operational record rather than being added to this public standard by default.

Treat silence as neutral. Do not follow up immediately.

## Governing rule

> **Distribution is part of the research loop, not a substitute for the research.**

Public synthesis should make existing work easier to encounter. Outreach should expose it to people with different vantage points. Feedback should improve the underlying thinking where warranted. The cadence succeeds when work becomes more inspectable, more challenged, and more connected to reality — not merely when it receives attention.
